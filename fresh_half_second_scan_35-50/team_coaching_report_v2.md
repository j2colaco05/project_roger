## Coaching summary

The team's effort and willingness to pursue the ball are clear. The largest opportunity is what the other five players do while one player handles the ball. Too often the shape contracts toward the contact, players rise out of their ready posture, and the next two contacts become improvised. Better spacing and earlier communication should improve both first-contact quality and the number of controlled attacks produced from ordinary rallies.

### Highest-priority improvements

1. **Own seams before contact.** Use early, loud calls: `mine`, `help`, `short`, `deep`, `line`, and `cross`. The passer should know the ball is theirs before it reaches the platform.
2. **Stay loaded until the rally is dead.** Use a split step at opponent contact, hips low, weight forward, and hands free. Several frames show upright defenders reacting after the ball has already changed direction.
3. **Use the rule: one plays, five prepare.** When one player contacts the ball, teammates should open a setter lane, release to attack, or form coverage. Do not all follow the ball.
4. **Build an attack from every playable dig.** Pass high toward the setting zone, identify the second-ball setter early, and provide at least two attacking options.
5. **Connect block and floor defense.** Back-row defenders must position around the block's line/cross decision instead of defending independently.

## Four-practice improvement plan

### Practice 1: Serve receive and seams

- 20 serves to three passers; a ball counts only when ownership is called before contact.
- Score 3 for a pass to target, 2 for a playable pass, 1 for an emergency ball, and 0 for an ace/overpass.
- Goal: 70% playable passes and zero silent seam errors.

### Practice 2: One plays, five prepare

- Run 6v6 wash rallies. Freeze immediately after first contact and check the other five players' roles.
- Require a named setter, two available attackers, and coverage before play resumes.
- Goal: produce a controlled third contact on 7 of 10 playable first balls.

### Practice 3: Block-defense connection

- Blockers call `line` or `cross` before the attack; defenders must visibly adjust around the call.
- Award the defense two points for a controlled dig and one for a playable pursuit.
- Goal: eliminate balls landing between a blocker and a stationary defender.

### Practice 4: Short-ball and scramble conversion

- Coach initiates tips, deflections, and imperfect digs. The team must call `mine`, `help`, and `setter out` as needed.
- Continue every rally until a whistle; no player may stop after the first save.
- Goal: turn 50% of emergency contacts into a controlled ball sent over with team coverage.

## Match-day communication card

Before every serve: name the seam, setter, and first defensive responsibility.

During every rally: `mine`, `help`, `short`, `deep`, `line`, `cross`, `setter out`.

After every point: one factual sentence, one adjustment, then reset. Example: `We left seam six open; left passer owns it next ball.`

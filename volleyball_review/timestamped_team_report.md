## Main Team Themes

1. Serve receive shape is often flat. The back-row passers frequently stand in almost the same horizontal line, which leaves unclear responsibility for seams, short balls, and middle-depth balls.
2. Ready posture needs to be lower and earlier. Several points begin with players upright or walking into position while the server is already preparing.
3. First contact is the biggest swing factor. Many rallies become difficult because the first ball does not go high and central enough for a controlled second touch.
4. Communication is late. Multiple plays show two players watching the same ball or a player hesitating because nobody clearly owns it.
5. Transition after first contact is slow. After a pass, dig, or free ball, players often stay where they contacted instead of moving into setter support, attack availability, or coverage.
6. Positive pattern: when the team starts lower and spaced, especially around the 9:00-9:30 and 16:00-16:30 stretches, the court coverage looks much better.

## Practice Priorities

1. Serve receive seam drill: three passers, coach/server sends balls between players; passer must call early and partner must release.
2. Free ball conversion: any easy ball must become pass to target, called second touch, controlled third ball to deep court.
3. Serve plus base: every serve is followed by an immediate sprint/step into defensive base.
4. Low-base habit: before every opponent contact, all players must be visibly low with weight forward.
5. Transition reset drill: after sending the ball over, everyone must spread to defensive shape within two steps.
